The manufacturer

Why this brand exists at all

The company started as a fiberglass manufacturer. It became an accessibility company because of one family problem.

In 1969 the founder, Gary Multanen, watched his own mother struggle to get over the wall of a bathtub. At that point there was no Home Depot, no Lowe's, and very few contractors who did this kind of work. He was already a manufacturer, so he and his engineering team solved it themselves. They built the first multi-piece barrier-free replacement shower — a unit designed to drop into the hole a bathtub leaves behind, carried in through a normal doorway in pieces.

That product created a category. Almost every tub-to-shower conversion sold in America today is a descendant of it.

The company is still family owned. It has never been bought by a conglomerate, which is unusual in this industry and shows up in the product: the line is deep, the tooling is old enough to be paid for, and they still build odd sizes because somebody asked.

Manufacturing runs out of Caldwell, Idaho and Sparta, Tennessee. Nothing is imported and assembled. The fiberglass structure, the assembly and the testing all happen in the same building.

What they invented, in order

  • The category

    Multi-piece barrier-free tub replacement

    A step-free shower that fits a bathtub footprint and carries in through a doorway. This is the product that made accessible remodeling possible in an existing house.

  • Water control

    The NWR collapsible water retainer

    A flexible rubber dam at the entry. It folds flat under a wheelchair or a foot and springs back to hold water in. It is the reason a half-inch threshold can work without flooding a bathroom.

  • Installation

    The low-profile, non-recessing pan

    A pan that does not need the subfloor cut away. It removed the single most expensive step from a barrier-free retrofit.

  • Assembly

    SnapJoint wall system

    A leak-proof locking joint between wall panels. One installer can close a corner without a second pair of hands and without relying on a bead of silicone to do structural work.

  • Retrofit

    Flange and window trim kits

    Trim that covers the cut edge where the old surround came off, so a conversion does not turn into a drywall job. There is also a 1¼″ flange trim engineered specifically for fire-rated new construction.

  • Job site

    TAS self-adhesive WaterStopper

    A peel-and-stick water dam. Faster containment, fewer callbacks.

Curbed and code-compliant showers

Not every project needs a half-inch entry. A curbed unit keeps water in with no effort and costs less to install, and for a steady adult it is a perfectly good choice.

The code-compliant range is a separate thing. Those units are dimensioned specifically to satisfy ADA, ANSI A117.1 Type A and Type B, and California Title 24 requirements. If you are building multifamily, senior housing, a hospital or student housing, this is the range that survives plan review.

ADA and commercial

Above-floor-rough showers

The answer to a concrete slab. An AFR unit keeps the drain body above the existing floor line and routes water out through a trench at the entry, so nobody has to cut the slab or core through a post-tension deck.

It is also the standard answer on upper floors, where cutting joists means an engineer and a change order. Threshold heights of 1″ and 2″ are available.

Where this saves real money

Slab cutting on a residential job commonly runs into the thousands once you include the saw, the spoil, the re-pour and the schedule. An AFR unit removes that line item.

Tub-shower combinations

For bathrooms that need to keep a tub, usually because of small children or a single-bathroom house. Available in general-use and code-compliant versions. Gelcoat surfaces clean more easily than tile, and the walls are wood-backed like everything else in the line.

Tub wall surrounds

Replaces failing tile above an existing tub without replacing the tub. Reinforced with wood and finished in a RealTile gelcoat that will support a grab bar or a wall-mounted seat with no additional blocking. Published sizes include 60×30, 60×32 and 46.5×27 inches.

Walk-in tubs

Tile patterns without tile

Beyond the stone looks, the gelcoat panels are molded in subway, 4-inch tile, 8-inch tile and smooth finishes. From two feet away they read as tile. Up close you can tell, because there is no grout line and no depth to the joint.

That trade is the whole point. Grout is where mold lives and where a tiled shower eventually fails. Removing it removes a maintenance job that lasts as long as the bathroom does.

Custom colors and real tile

Stock gelcoat colors are the economical route, and color-matched silicone ships with multi-piece units so the seams disappear. Beyond stock, custom colors, accent bands and backsplashes are available.

Real tile can also be integrated into a unit, which is the option people reach for when a designer is involved and the shower has to match something specific. See what custom involves.

Labor

The install is where the savings are

A published comparison study found that installing a one-piece shower cost 82% less than ceramic tile and 30% less than a conventional mud-set surround.

Those figures are the manufacturer's, so weigh them accordingly. But the mechanism behind them is not controversial. A tiled shower needs waterproofing, a float, a set, a grout, a cure and a seal. Five trades' worth of sequencing. A molded unit needs a level floor, a drain connection and a day.

For a contractor running multiple units, that difference compounds into a schedule advantage, not just a price one.

Choosing

Which part of the line is yours

The catalog is large, which is a strength once you know where to start and a problem before that. Four questions narrow it to one or two products.

1. What is the entry height you need?

Half an inch puts you in the barrier-free range. One to two inches puts you in low-threshold. Four inches puts you in curbed. If the project has to pass plan review, you are in the code-compliant range regardless of the number.

This is the question that eliminates the most products, which is why it goes first.

2. What is under the floor?

Concrete slab, or an upstairs bathroom over a finished ceiling, pushes you toward above-floor rough. Ground floor over a crawl space or an open basement keeps the whole range available, including a properly recessed barrier-free pan.

3. How does it get into the bathroom?

Tight access means multi-piece. Open framing or new construction allows single-piece, which is seamless and stronger. There is no third option here and it is not a matter of preference.

4. Walls, pan, or both?

If your walls are sound and only the surround has failed, a tub wall surround or a Crestline wall system solves it in a day. If the floor is the problem, a pan alone with tile above it is a legitimate and common answer. A complete kit is for when both need replacing, which is most conversions.

Answer those four and we can usually name the product on the first call.

Bestbath questions we get

Are you an authorized Bestbath dealer?

Yes. We are an independent dealer and we can order anything in the catalog, including custom dimensions and custom colors. Best Shower Kit is not owned by or operated by the manufacturer.

Where are Bestbath products made?

Caldwell, Idaho and Sparta, Tennessee. The fiberglass structure, assembly and testing all happen domestically. Nothing is imported and re-badged, which also means warranty claims go to a US manufacturer rather than an importer.

What does the 30-year warranty actually cover?

The manufacturer warrants that the fiberglass-reinforced showers, walls and pans will be free from manufacturing defects for 30 years from the date of installation, and that units will be delivered free of freight damage. Read the full warranty text before you buy; we will send it to you with the quote.

Can I get a size that is not in the catalog?

Yes. Custom pan sizes, custom wall dimensions and complete custom kits are built to order. This is one of the main reasons people choose this manufacturer over a stocked brand. Send us the three measurements and we will price it.

How long does delivery take?

Most fiberglass units ship in roughly five to seven business days. Accessories generally ship the next business day, subject to availability. Selected in-stock configurations ship faster. Certain valve and fixture brands carry longer lead times and we will flag those before you order.

Can a Bestbath unit go into a bathroom on the second floor?

Usually yes, and the above-floor-rough range exists specifically for that case. The question to answer first is whether the drain can be reached without cutting joists. If it cannot, an AFR unit with a trench threshold solves it without structural work.

Do you install, or just supply?

Both, depending on where you are. Tell us your ZIP code and what you need, and we will either quote the installation or supply the kit and give your contractor the spec sheet and install instructions.

How do I clean a gelcoat shower without wrecking it?

Non-abrasive liquid cleaner, a soft cloth or sponge, and nothing with grit in it. Avoid scouring powders, steel wool and heavy bleach routines. Done that way, a heavy gelcoat surface keeps its gloss for decades. Done with an abrasive pad, it will haze within a couple of years.
