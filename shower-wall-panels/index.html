Shower wall panels

Grout is the reason bathrooms look tired after five years. It stains, it cracks, and it grows things nobody wants to think about. These panels give you the look of tile and natural stone in a surface you clean with a sponge and never seal again.

The short answer

Three ranges. Triple gel coat is the hardest, deepest finish we build. Natural stone style reproduces marble, slate and limestone. PVC tile style gives you subway and tile patterns with no grout lines in them. A plain smooth finish is also available and is the value option.

All of them are solid surface with full reinforced backing, so grab bars mount at the height that suits the person rather than wherever a stud lands. Choose a single backwall for a seamless look, or a split backwall if the panels have to get up a staircase.

Beyond the standard range we match custom colors, mould accent bands and backsplashes into the surround, and integrate real tile where you want it.

Single or split

The choice that decides whether it reaches the bathroom.

This looks like an aesthetic decision and is actually a logistics one. Get it wrong and the panels arrive but cannot physically get up your stairs.

 Single backwallSplit backwall
AppearanceOne uninterrupted panel, no vertical seam behind the fixturesA single vertical joint down the back wall, sealed on install
Getting it inNeeds a clear, mostly straight path from the entranceClears standard doorways, stair turns and tight landings
Best forGround-floor bathrooms, new builds, gut renovationsUpstairs bathrooms, apartments, older homes with narrow halls
HandlingTwo people and some planningConsiderably easier for one or two people
CleaningNothing at all across the back wallOne sealed joint, wiped like any other surface

How to decide in thirty seconds: measure the narrowest point between your front door and the bathroom — usually a doorway or the turn at the top of a staircase. If that number worries you at all, choose split. The sealed joint is genuinely unobtrusive, and it is a much better outcome than a panel stuck on a landing.

Panels versus tile

An honest comparison, including where tile wins.

We sell panels, so treat this with appropriate suspicion. But there are real cases where tile is the right answer, and you should know what they are before you spend anything.

 Solid surface panelsCeramic or stone tile
GroutNone anywhere. Nothing to reseal, ever.Needs periodic sealing, and stains and cracks over time.
Installation timeTypically one day, one crew.Six to ten working days across several trades.
CostSubstantially lower, mostly because factory waterproofing replaces on-site labour.Substantially higher. Labour is 40 to 60 percent of the job.
CleaningSponge and ordinary bathroom cleaner.Grout brushing, mildew treatment, resealing.
Grab bar mountingFull reinforced backing, so bars go anywhere.Blocking must be planned before the tile goes on.
Design freedomWide, but within a defined range of finishes and formats.Effectively unlimited. This is where tile genuinely wins.
RepairPanels are replaced as a section.Individual tiles can be swapped, if you kept spares.

When we would tell you to tile: if the design is the whole point — a specific handmade tile, a mosaic, an unusual geometry — and the budget and timeline can carry it. For everyone else, and particularly for anyone who wants a shower that still looks new in ten years with no maintenance, panels are the better purchase. And if you want a bit of both, we integrate real tile into the panel system.

Wall panel questions

What people ask about walls.

Call 1-888-779-2284 and we will help you choose a finish that suits the room.

What are shower wall panels made of?

Our wall systems are craftsman-built from reinforced fiberglass with a polyester gelcoat finish, in triple gel coat, natural stone style and PVC tile style ranges. All of them are solid surface, which means no grout lines anywhere and nothing to reseal. They are made in the USA and carry a 30-year limited warranty.

Are shower wall panels better than tile?

For most bathrooms, yes, and grout is the reason. Grout stains, cracks and harbours mildew, which is why tiled showers start looking tired after about five years and need periodic resealing. A panel system wipes clean with a sponge and ordinary bathroom cleaner. Tile still wins on total design freedom, which is why we also integrate real tile into panel systems when someone wants a specific look.

What is the difference between triple gel coat and PVC tile style panels?

Triple gel coat refers to the depth of the finish built up on the panel, giving the hardest, most durable surface we make and the richest color. PVC tile style panels are moulded with a tile pattern such as subway, 4 inch or 8 inch, so you get the look of tile without any grout. Natural stone style sits alongside both, reproducing marble, slate and limestone.

What finishes and colors are available?

Stone-look finishes include white marble, grey marble, grey slate and limestone. Tile patterns include subway, 4 inch tile and 8 inch tile. A smooth finish is available and is the value option. Beyond the standard range we match custom colors, so panels can be coordinated with a vanity, a floor tile, or a color you have already chosen.

What is the difference between a single backwall and a split backwall?

A single backwall is one uninterrupted panel with no vertical seam behind the fixtures. A split backwall arrives as two narrower sections. The difference matters for access rather than looks: split panels fit up tight stairwells, around corners and through doorways a full sheet will not clear. If your bathroom is upstairs or down a hallway with a turn in it, choose split.

Can real tile be combined with the wall panels?

Yes. We can work genuine tile or stone into the wall system, including accent bands and a backsplash moulded into the surround rather than stuck on afterward. That gives you a real tile look in the places that show, with an easy-clean solid surface everywhere else and far less grout to maintain.

Can I buy wall panels without a shower pan?

Yes. If your existing base is sound and the tile around it has failed, replacing only the walls saves considerable money and disruption. Panels are available in two-piece, three-piece and split backwall configurations to suit the opening you already have.

Do the wall panels support grab bars?

Yes, and this is one of the most useful things about them. Full reinforced backing runs through the panels, so a grab bar can be mounted at the height that suits the person using the shower rather than wherever a stud happens to fall. If someone's needs change later, the wall is already prepared.

How tall are the wall panels?

Standard wall systems run to roughly 80 to 82 inches tall depending on configuration. If you have a low ceiling, a sloped ceiling or a soffit in the way, we make custom wall heights, so measure floor to ceiling inside the alcove and give us the number.

How do you clean solid surface shower walls?

A sponge and ordinary bathroom cleaner. There is no grout to scrub and nothing to reseal. Avoid abrasive pads and scouring powders, because those will dull the gelcoat over time. That is genuinely the whole maintenance routine.

Will the wall panels fit through my door?

Panels are supplied in sections rather than as one piece, and a split backwall breaks the largest panel into two narrower ones specifically so it can clear doorways, stair turns and tight landings. Measure the narrowest point on the route from your entrance to the bathroom and tell us that number.

Do I need to remove the old tile first?

In most cases yes, because the panels need to sit against a sound, flat substrate and the finished shower needs to end up the right size. Your installer will assess what is behind the tile at the same time, which is worth doing anyway since a shower that has been leaking will usually have damage nobody has seen.
