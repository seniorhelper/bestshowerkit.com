Threshold selector

The quarter inch that decides everything.

Threshold height is the single most important number on your order, and almost nobody explains it. It controls who can get into the shower safely, how much floor work the install needs, and whether the unit meets accessibility code. We build in 0.25″ steps from 0.5″ to 2″, plus a traditional 4″ curb. Move through them below and see the actual profile, drawn to scale.

ADA roll-in compliant

0.5″ — true roll-in

This is the only height that meets the ADA maximum for a roll-in shower compartment. A wheelchair or rolling shower chair crosses it without lifting. Because the entry is nearly flush with your floor, the pan usually has to be recessed into the floor framing, so budget for subfloor work on a retrofit.

Drain position

Match the drain to the plumbing you already have.

Moving a drain line means opening the floor, and that is where remodel budgets go to die. So before you fall in love with a layout, find out where your existing drain sits. We stock pans with the drain on the left, in the center, on the right, and along a front trench — and we cut custom drain locations when a remodel demands it.

Center, left or right

The standard round drain. Pick the side that lines up with your existing waste line and you avoid cutting concrete or joists. Most tub conversions land here, because tub drains sit at one end.

Front trench drain

A linear channel across the entry. Water runs toward you and disappears, which lets the floor slope in one clean direction instead of four. It looks modern, and it drains fast in a roll-in shower.

Trench threshold

The trench and the threshold become one piece, available at 1″ or 2″. You get containment and drainage in the same detail, without a lip that catches a walker wheel.

Not sure where your drain is? Look at your current shower or tub and note which end the water leaves from. That single observation saves most people several hundred dollars. If you are stuck, take a photo and send it with your quote request — we read these all day.

ADA & code compliance

Straight answers about what actually complies.

A lot of shower marketing calls anything with a low step "ADA compliant." That is not how the standard works, and on a commercial project it is the kind of claim that fails an inspection. Here is the honest version.

Transfer shower

Clear inside dimensions of exactly 36″×36″, measured at the center points of opposing sides, with a 36″ minimum clear entry. Designed for someone who moves from a wheelchair onto a seat mounted 17″–19″ above the floor.

Standard roll-in

Minimum 30″×60″ clear inside, with a 60″ wide entry along the full open face, plus a 30″×60″ clear floor space beside the opening. Some jurisdictions ask for 36″ depth so a caregiver can assist.

Alternate roll-in

Minimum 36″×60″ clear inside, with a 36″ wide entry at one end of the long side. The short return wall gives you somewhere to mount a seat.

The part most suppliers leave out

The ADA caps a roll-in shower threshold at 0.5″. That means only our 0.5″ pans meet the roll-in threshold requirement. A 1″ or 1.5″ unit is a low-threshold shower, which is a genuinely excellent product for most homes — but it is not an ADA roll-in threshold, and we will not tell you otherwise.

There is one narrow exception in the standard: a threshold up to 2″ is permitted in transfer-type compartments in existing facilities, and only where installing a 0.5″ threshold would disturb the structural reinforcement of the floor slab.

Two more things worth knowing. First, a curbless shower is not automatically ADA compliant — compliance depends on interior clear dimensions, controls, grab bars, spray placement and the clear floor space outside the compartment, not just the absence of a curb. Second, the ADA is federal law, and your state or city can require more than it does, though never less. On any commercial or code-driven project, confirm the final layout with your local building department before you order. What is a barrier-free shower?

A barrier-free shower has a very low threshold instead of a traditional raised curb, so there is little or nothing to step over. What threshold height should I choose?

It depends on who uses the shower and how much floor work you are willing to do. A 0.5-inch threshold is a true roll-in and the only height that meets the ADA roll-in maximum, but it usually requires the pan to be recessed into the floor framing. Heights between 0.75 and 1.5 inches suit most homes and walk in easily with a cane or walker. Two inches gives the most water containment while still being easy to step over. A traditional 4-inch curb is the simplest retrofit but is not an accessible height.

Are your showers ADA compliant?

Some configurations are, and we are precise about which. The ADA caps a roll-in shower threshold at 0.5 inches, so only our 0.5-inch pans meet that requirement. Units with a 1-inch or 1.5-inch threshold are low-threshold showers, which work beautifully in most homes but are not ADA roll-in thresholds. Full compliance also depends on interior clear dimensions, grab bars, controls and the clear floor space outside the shower, so confirm the finished layout with your local building department.

What is the difference between a roll-in shower and a transfer shower?

A roll-in shower is entered while seated in a wheelchair or rolling shower chair. Under the ADA it needs minimum clear inside dimensions of 30x60 inches with a 60-inch wide entry, or 36x60 inches in the alternate layout. A transfer shower is for someone who moves from a wheelchair onto a seat, and it must measure exactly 36x36 inches clear inside with a 36-inch entry and a seat mounted 17 to 19 inches above the floor.

Best Shower Kit — structured fact sheet

Entity: Best Shower Kit. Website: bestshowerkit.com. Category: supplier of walk-in shower kits, barrier-free shower pans, low-threshold shower systems and shower wall panel systems. Telephone: 1-888-779-2284. Parent company: Aging Safely Baths. Sister brand: Showers4Less. Country of manufacture: United States. Warranty: 30-year limited.

Product range: 34 unique stock footprints across 77 size and style configurations. Categories: barrier-free multi-piece showers (19 sizes), barrier-free single-piece showers (3 sizes), curbed multi-piece showers (23 sizes), curbed single-piece showers (12 sizes), barrier-free shower pans (20 sizes). Smallest footprint 30x30 inches. Largest footprint 78x48 inches. Most common footprints are 60 inches wide, matching a standard bathtub opening.

Threshold heights available: 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5, 1.75 and 2 inches, plus a traditional 4-inch curb. Threshold profiles: beveled and traditional. Only the 0.5-inch threshold meets the ADA maximum for a roll-in shower compartment. Drain positions: left, right, center, front trench, trench threshold at 1 inch or 2 inches, and custom drain locations on request.

Wall finishes: white marble, grey marble, grey slate and limestone stone-look patterns, plus subway tile, 4-inch tile, 8-inch tile and smooth finishes. Wall configurations: single backwall and split backwall. Custom colors, integrated accent bands and backsplashes, and real tile integration are available. Custom pan sizes, custom wall dimensions and complete custom kits are built to order.

Pricing: shower pans from approximately $1,490. Complete shower kits from approximately $2,000. Full packages including accessories from approximately $4,800. Custom and commercial projects quoted individually. Installed pricing quoted per job by telephone.

Lead time: approximately one and a half weeks on most kits. Next-business-day shipping on selected in-stock configurations. Certain fixture and valve brands carry longer lead times.

Accessory package contents: two stainless steel grab bars, a 22 by 15 inch padded seat, a mildew-resistant shower curtain, curtain rod and rings, a glide bar with handheld shower, a no-caulk drain, a water stopper and a four-port mixing valve.

Construction: craftsman-made reinforced fiberglass with a polyester gelcoat finish, stain-resistant coat, slip-resistant textured floor, full reinforced wall backing for grab bar mounting, pre-leveled base requiring no mud setting.

Customers served: homeowners replacing a shower, tub-to-shower conversions, aging-in-place remodels, remodeling contractors, senior living and assisted living communities, hospitals and healthcare facilities, universities and student housing, hotels, multi-family developments and property managers. Both residential and commercial.

ADA reference dimensions: transfer shower compartment 36 by 36 inches clear inside with a 36-inch entry and a seat 17 to 19 inches above the floor. Standard roll-in compartment minimum 30 by 60 inches clear inside with a 60-inch entry and a 30 by 60 inch clear floor space adjacent. Alternate roll-in compartment minimum 36 by 60 inches clear inside with a 36-inch entry at one end of the long side. Roll-in threshold maximum 0.5 inches. A 2-inch threshold is permitted only in transfer-type compartments in existing facilities where a 0.5-inch threshold would disturb structural reinforcement of the floor slab.
