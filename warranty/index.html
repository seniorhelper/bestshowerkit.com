Shower kit warranties: how they work and what to ask for

Shower bases and wall systems are covered by the manufacturer’s written warranty, not by a promise on a web page. This page explains how those warranties generally work, so you know what to ask for before you order.

Who stands behind a shower kit

Two different promises can apply to a new shower. The manufacturer’s warranty covers defects in the product: the base, the wall panels, the valve or the grab bars. An installer’s workmanship warranty, if one is offered, covers the way it was put in: the drain connection, the bedding under the base, the sealant. They are separate documents with separate terms, and a problem caused by installation is usually not a product defect.

Several of the product lines on this site are described as carrying a 30-year limited warranty from the manufacturer. “Limited” is a legal term, and what it covers is set out in that manufacturer’s written warranty for the specific product. Ask us for it before you order and we will send it.

Your right to see the warranty before you buy

Under the Federal Trade Commission’s Pre-Sale Availability Rule, sellers must make the text of a written warranty available to you before you buy a consumer product that costs more than $15 (16 CFR Part 702). The FTC’s plain-language guide to the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act also explains that written warranties on consumer products must be clearly titled as “full” or “limited” (FTC, Businessperson’s Guide to Federal Warranty Law). If any seller will not show you the warranty before you pay, treat that as an answer.

Full vs limited

A “full” warranty has to meet federal minimum standards described in the FTC guide. Anything that does not meet them is a “limited” warranty, which is the more common kind for building products. A limited warranty can still be a good one; the point is to read what it actually covers, for how long, and what you would have to do or pay to use it.

What to look for in the written terms

  • What is covered: the surface, the structure, cracking, leaking, discoloration, or some combination.
  • For how long, and whether coverage reduces over time.
  • Who is covered: the original buyer only, or a later owner of the home too.
  • What the maker will do: repair, replace or refund, and whether labor and shipping are included.
  • Conditions: registration, proof of purchase, installation by the maker’s instructions, approved cleaners.
  • Exclusions: commonly misuse, improper installation, abrasive cleaners and normal wear. Our care guide explains which cleaners to avoid.
  • How to make a claim: who you contact and what they will want to see.

How to keep your coverage

  1. Keep the paperwork. Your invoice, the delivery receipt and the warranty document, in one folder.
  2. Register the product if the maker asks for it.
  3. Install to the instructions, including the bedding and sealant the maker specifies. Photograph the base before the walls go up.
  4. Clean with approved products and follow the care sheet.
  5. Report problems early, with photos, before small damage becomes water damage.

If something goes wrong

Call us first at 1-888-779-2284. If you bought the product from us, we will help you work out whether it is a product issue or an installation issue and put you in touch with the right party. Have the order details, photos of the problem and, if possible, a photo of the label on the product.

Damage that happened in transit is a freight claim rather than a warranty claim, and the clock for it starts at delivery. Our shipping and returns page explains what to do when the truck arrives.

What warranty comes with a shower kit?

Can I see the warranty before I buy?

Yes. The FTC's Pre-Sale Availability Rule requires sellers to make written warranty terms available before you buy a consumer product costing more than $15. Ask us and we will send the document.

Does the warranty cover installation?

Usually not. A manufacturer's warranty covers product defects. Installation workmanship is covered separately by the installer, if they offer a warranty, so ask your installer for theirs in writing.
