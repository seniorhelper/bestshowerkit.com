How much does a walk-in shower kit cost?

Most articles answering this question quote a remodel average and leave you no wiser. This page does the opposite. Here are the actual component prices, what each upgrade adds, what installation costs on top, and where the money genuinely goes.

The short answer

Buying the product on its own, expect $1,490–$2,100 for a barrier-free shower pan, $2,000–$4,500 for a complete kit with matched pan and wall system, and $4,800–$6,700 for a full package that includes grab bars, a padded seat, a glide bar with handheld shower, a mixing valve and the trim.

Add professional installation and a prefabricated walk-in shower generally lands between $4,000 and $7,000 all in, according to independent 2026 national data. A custom tile shower doing the same job runs $14,000 to $20,000, because you are paying several trades to build waterproofing on site that a factory already built for you.

Three things move your number more than anything else: whether you need only the pan or the whole system, whether your existing drain is in the right place, and whether you install it yourself.

The bottom of those published ranges is a basic curbed fiberglass stall or pan, often without a door, a valve or anything to hold onto. Price drivers

Six things that move your number.

Understand these and you can steer the price deliberately rather than discovering it at the end. Two of them are worth real money and cost you nothing but a tape measure.

Drain position

The biggest avoidable expense in the whole project. If you order a pan whose drain matches your existing waste line, the plumber connects and leaves. If it does not match, someone opens the floor, and on a concrete slab that is a serious job.

Matching your drain saves several hundred to a few thousand dollars

Threshold height

The pan itself costs about the same at any height. What changes is the floor work. A 0.5″ roll-in threshold usually needs recessing into the framing. Anything from 0.75″ upward generally sits on the subfloor with minimal preparation.

Recessing a pan typically adds $400–$1,200 in labour

Wall finish

A smooth finish is the value option and genuinely looks clean. Tile-look patterns in subway, 4″ and 8″ cost more to tool. Stone-look finishes in marble, slate and limestone sit at the top.

Tile-look adds roughly $600–$800; stone-look roughly $1,000–$1,200

Size

More material, more money, but the curve is gentler than people expect. Going from a 60×30 to a 60×36 is a modest step. Jumping to 60×48 or 72×48 is where it becomes noticeable.

A large footprint adds roughly 15–20% over standard

Accessories

Grab bars, a padded seat, a glide bar and a mixing valve add up quickly, but they are the parts that make the shower usable for the person it was bought for. Buy them as a package rather than piecemeal.

Full accessory package $1,300–$1,580

Custom work

Custom dimensions, custom colors, integrated backsplashes and real tile all carry a premium. It is usually still far below a fully tiled build, and it is the only way to solve a bathroom that does not match any catalogue size.

Quoted individually — ask before you assume it is out of reach

The comparison that matters

Why a kit costs a third of a tiled shower.

It is not that the materials are cheaper. It is that the waterproofing was done in a factory. A tiled shower is built waterproof on site, by hand, by several trades, over two weeks. Every one of those hours is on your invoice.

Installed cost, prefabricated kit versus custom tile

Independent 2026 national estimates for a finished, professionally installed walk-in shower.

Source: This Old House, 2026 walk-in shower cost analysis. Angi reports a national average near $9,000 across all shower types, with a typical range of $6,000 to $12,000. HomeGuide puts labour at 40 to 60 percent of a tiled shower installation, which is the gap in the chart above.

Where the tile money goes

Demolition crew, waterproofing membrane, mortar bed, tile setter, grout, sealing, plus a plumber and often an electrician coordinating around all of it. Six to ten working days with your only bathroom out of action, and every additional trade is another chance for a delay.

Where the kit money goes

A pre-leveled pan that needs no mortar bed, reinforced wall panels with the backing already in them, and one crew for one day. The waterproofing arrived finished. There is no grout to seal now or re-seal in three years.

Installation

What installation actually adds.

For a straightforward swap where the drain already lines up, professional installation typically adds $1,800 to $3,200 on top of the product. That covers removing and disposing of the old unit, setting the pan, hanging the walls, connecting the drain and fitting the trim.

It climbs from there when the job asks for more. Moving a drain line, recessing a pan for a low threshold, repairing a rotted subfloor, or relocating a valve all add labour. None of those are surprises if someone looks properly before quoting, which is exactly why we quote installation individually rather than publishing a flat rate.

Doing it yourself removes that entire line. These kits were designed for it: the pan arrives pre-leveled so there is no mortar bed, and instructions and materials come in the box. A confident DIYer with basic plumbing skills can finish a straightforward replacement in a day.

Budget honestly

The costs people forget until the invoice arrives.

None of these are hidden fees. They are simply the parts of a bathroom project that sit outside the shower itself, and they catch people out with impressive consistency.

Freight

These ship as oversized freight, not parcel. Cost depends on your ZIP code and whether the delivery needs a lift gate or residential access. We quote it up front, always.

Disposal

A cast-iron tub is genuinely heavy and most municipal collections will not take it. Budget for a skip or a haul-away, or confirm it is included in your installer's quote.

The valve

If your existing valve stays, you save money. If it is old or in the wrong place, it gets replaced, and certain fixture brands carry longer lead times than the shower itself.

Subfloor repair

Nobody knows what is under a shower until the old one comes out. If a pan has been leaking quietly for years, there will be repair work. Hold a contingency.

Permits

A like-for-like swap often needs none. Move plumbing or alter framing and it usually does. Your local building department will tell you in one phone call.

Finishing the room

A new shower makes tired flooring and a dated vanity look worse. That is not a cost of the shower, but it is a cost people end up meeting anyway, so decide early.

Spend less

Five ways to cut the cost without cutting the shower.

We would rather sell you the right thing at the right price than the most expensive thing once. These are the levers that genuinely work, in the order they save the most.

1. Match your existing drain

Worth more than every other saving combined. Look at your current shower or tub, note which end the water leaves from, and order a pan with the drain in that position. Free to do, and it can keep a plumber out of your floor entirely.

2. Buy only what has failed

A cracked pan under sound walls does not require a whole new system. Buying the pan alone can cut the product cost by well over half. Ask us honestly what needs replacing and we will tell you honestly.

3. Choose a smooth finish

A smooth wall panel looks clean, modern and bright, and it costs several hundred dollars less than a tile-look or stone-look pattern. It is also marginally easier to wipe down, having no moulded relief at all.

4. Pick a threshold you can afford to install

If nobody in the house uses a wheelchair, a 0.75″ or 1″ threshold gives almost all the accessibility benefit of a roll-in without the floor recessing that a 0.5″ pan usually demands.

5. Ask what is in stock

Selected configurations are held in stock and ship the next business day. If your timing is flexible on finish or drain position, an in-stock unit can save you both money and a week and a half of waiting.

One thing we would not cut

Grab bars. They are a small share of the total and they are the reason the shower is safe. Our wall panels have full reinforced backing throughout, so bars can be mounted at the height that suits the person, not the framing — and moved later if needs change.

How much is just a shower pan?

Is a walk-in shower cheaper than a tiled shower?

Considerably. A prefabricated kit installs for roughly a third of what a custom tile shower costs, because the waterproofing is done in the factory rather than built by hand on site. HomeGuide reports that labour accounts for 40 to 60 percent of a tiled shower installation, and that labour is what disappears when you buy a kit.

What is the cheapest way to get a walk-in shower?

Match your existing drain position, buy only the component that has failed, choose a smooth wall finish, pick a threshold height that does not require recessing the pan, and ask what is currently in stock. Matching your drain is worth more than all the others combined, and it costs nothing but a look at your existing shower.

Why does the drain position affect the price so much?

Because moving a drain means opening the floor. On a suspended timber floor that is disruptive; on a concrete slab it means cutting concrete, which is a serious job with serious labour attached. Ordering a pan with the drain where your waste line already sits avoids the whole exercise.

Does a lower threshold cost more?

The pan itself costs about the same at any threshold height. What changes is the installation. A 0.5 inch roll-in threshold usually requires the pan to be recessed into the floor framing, which typically adds $400 to $1,200 in labour. Heights from 0.75 inches upward generally sit on the subfloor with minimal preparation.

How much does a tub-to-shower conversion cost?

Typically $4,500 to $8,500 all in with professional installation. A standard alcove tub is 60 inches long, so a 60 inch wide pan usually drops into the footprint the tub leaves behind. Because tub drains sit at one end rather than the center, matching your drain position matters more on this job than almost any other.

How much does an ADA roll-in shower cost?

Usually $7,500 to $14,000 installed. The ADA caps a roll-in threshold at 0.5 inches, which means the pan has to sit nearly flush with the finished floor. On most retrofits that requires structural floor work, and on a concrete slab that single factor is the biggest variable in the estimate.
